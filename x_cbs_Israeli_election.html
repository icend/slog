Israeli election exit polls show race headed toward stalemate, negotiations on forming government.
​
Exit polls from Israel's major television networks project that a majority of Israelis voted against Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's coalition in Tuesday's election, but his Likud party still emerged as the largest party among all the contenders. Neither bloc, Netanyahu or the opposition, has a clear majority. 
​
While Netanyahu appears to have won fewer votes than his main challenger, according to the early exit polls, both candidates will likely struggle to cobble together a coalition.
​
One or the other would need to obtain majority support in Israel's 120-seat parliament, known as the Knesset, in order to form a government.
​
Israel's two Arab parties won more seats in parliament than in any previous election. That electoral success comes after what some Arab voters told CBS News was a difficult time for them in the country under the hard-right government of Netanyahu, prompting a historic turnout for the minority community. 
​
Both Netanyahu and those opposing him have vowed not to form a government reliant on Arab parties, arguing it would endanger the country if they gain enough seats to influence Israel's engagement in its myriad conflicts. Arab citizens of Israel are around 20% of the population.
​
If the opposition breaks their preelection vow — a common occurrence in Israeli politics — they could take power and remove Netanyahu. A central tenet of Netanyahu's election campaign was keeping the Arab parties out of power. 
​
This is the first election since the Oct. 7, 2023, attacks by Hamas, which authorities have acknowledged as Israel's most catastrophic security failure since the country's founding in 1948. Israelis headed to the polls with their country still fighting in the subsequent multifront war in Gaza, Lebanon and Syria, and after two direct rounds of conflict with Iran and Netanyahu advocating for renewed strikes.
​
For many Israelis, after three years of war on multiple fronts, the election is seen as existential, and voter turnout appeared to be high.
​
Netanyahu vs. the "change bloc"
Netanyahu, Israel's longest-serving prime minister and leader of the Likud party, is seeking to form another coalition similar to his current government, which relies on partnerships with ultranationalist and Jewish religious parties.
​
Netanyahu is telling voters that Israel's campaigns in Gaza, Lebanon and especially Iran, are ongoing, and he needs more time to finish what his government started.

中文

以色列选举出口民调显示，选情陷入僵局，组阁谈判即将展开。

以色列各大电视网的出口民调显示，在周二的选举中，多数以色列选民投票反对总理本雅明·内塔尼亚胡（Benjamin Netanyahu）的执政联盟；不过，他的利库德集团（Likud party）仍是各参选政党中规模最大的一党。无论是内塔尼亚胡阵营还是反对派阵营，目前均未获得绝对多数席位。

根据初步出口民调，尽管内塔尼亚胡获得的票数似乎少于其主要竞争对手，但双方在组建联合政府的过程中，恐怕都将面临重重困难。

任何一方若想成功组建政府，都必须在以色列议会（Knesset，共120个席位）中获得多数支持。

以色列的两个阿拉伯政党此次在议会中赢得的席位，超过了以往任何一届选举。一些阿拉伯裔选民向美国哥伦比亚广播公司（CBS News）表示，在内塔尼亚胡极右翼政府的统治下，他们度过了一段艰难时期；这种处境促使该少数族裔群体创下了历史性的投票率。

内塔尼亚胡及其反对者均曾誓言，绝不会组建一个依赖阿拉伯政党的政府。他们声称，如果阿拉伯政党获得足够席位，从而能够影响以色列在多重冲突中的立场，将危及国家安全。以色列境内的阿拉伯裔公民约占总人口的20%。

如果反对派违背选前承诺——这在以色列政坛并不罕见——他们就有可能夺取政权，将内塔尼亚胡赶下台。内塔尼亚胡此次竞选的核心主张之一，就是将阿拉伯政党排除在权力核心之外。

这是自2023年10月7日哈马斯发动袭击以来的首次选举。当局已承认，那次袭击是以色列自1948年建国以来最严重的安全灾难。在本次选举中，以色列正深陷加沙、黎巴嫩和叙利亚的多线战争；此外，以色列此前还与伊朗发生了两轮直接冲突，而内塔尼亚胡目前正主张对伊朗再次发动打击。

对许多以色列人而言，在经历了三年的多线战争后，这场选举关乎国家存亡，投票率似乎相当高。内塔尼亚胡与“变革阵营”的较量 内塔尼亚胡是以色列在任时间最长的总理，也是利库德集团的领导人。他正寻求组建另一个类似于其现任政府的执政联盟——该联盟依赖于与极端民族主义政党及犹太宗教政党的合作。

内塔尼亚胡向选民表示，以色列在加沙、黎巴嫩，尤其是针对伊朗的行动仍在进行之中，他需要更多时间来完成其政府已经开启的各项任务。
